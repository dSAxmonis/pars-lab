Experiment 1: Study of Datasets Across Different Domains (CIFAR-10 & Iris)

Pattern Analysis & Recognition Systems Lab  |  Repository: https://github.com/dSAxmonis/pars-lab

AIM

To conduct an in-depth comparative academic study of two benchmark datasets belonging to fundamentally different modalities and application domains: 1) CIFAR-10 (High-dimensional computer vision image recognition domain) and 2) Iris Flower Dataset (Low-dimensional botanical morphological tabular classification domain), evaluating their purpose, source, feature representations, mathematical dimensionality, state-of-the-art work by various authors, recent performance benchmarks, and formal citations.

SECTION 2: FEATURE DESCRIPTIONS & MODALITY INSPECTION

CIFAR-10 Feature Architecture ($d = 3,072$)

  • Spatial Resolution: $32 \times 32$ raster matrix (1,024 pixels per channel).
  • Color Depth: 3 channels (Red, Green, Blue) $\rightarrow$ 3D tensor $I \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 32 \times 3}$.
  • Feature Scale: 8-bit unsigned integers $[0, 255]$ representing photon intensity.
  • Inductive Prior: Strong 2D spatial locality and translation equivariance.
Total Features = 32 (height) × 32 (width) × 3 (RGB) = 3,072 continuous values

Iris Tabular Architecture ($d = 4$)

  • Feature 1 ($x_1$): Sepal Length in cm (continuous ratio scale, range $4.3 - 7.9$).
  • Feature 2 ($x_2$): Sepal Width in cm (continuous ratio scale, range $2.0 - 4.4$).
  • Feature 3 ($x_3$): Petal Length in cm (continuous ratio scale, range $1.0 - 6.9$).
  • Feature 4 ($x_4$): Petal Width in cm (continuous ratio scale, range $0.1 - 2.5$).
Total Features = 4 morphological continuous geometric measurements

SECTION 4: CROSS-DOMAIN COMPARATIVE SYNTHESIS & ANALYTICAL SUMMARY
Analytical Dimension Computer Vision Domain (CIFAR-10) Botanical Tabular Domain (Iris)
Curse of Dimensionality High feature dimension ($d=3072$) mandates tens of thousands of samples to prevent empty space phenomena. Compact feature space ($d=4$) allows dense data coverage with only $N=150$ observations.
Spatial Correlation & Locality Adjacent pixels exhibit high covariance; spatial context and translation invariance are paramount. Features are distinct physical measurements with no topological ordering or spatial grid proximity.
Feature Engineering Needs Requires automatic hierarchical feature representation via convolutional or self-attention layers. Features are pre-engineered domain metrics directly interpretable by human botanists.
Sample & Compute Efficiency Requires GPU acceleration, extensive optimization epochs, and millions of parameters. Sub-second CPU training via closed-form solutions (LDA, ELM) or lightweight SVMs.
