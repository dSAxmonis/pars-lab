Practical Name

Multimodal Feature Extraction from Audio Across Multiple Timestamps Using Flask

Aim

To prepare an informative web-based application using Flask to perform automated feature extraction from multimedia signals (audio). Specifically, to extract at least three distinctive time-domain and frequency-domain acoustic features across at least 10 discrete timestamps, justify their technical objectives for downstream machine learning applications, and provide an embedded audio player for real-time verification.

Definitions & Core Concepts

Non-Stationary Signal Framing: Continuous audio signals are non-stationary over extended intervals but exhibit quasi-stationary physical properties across short analysis frames (20–50 ms). Temporal windowing isolates discrete segments for localized feature computation.

Time-Domain Acoustic Features: Derived directly from instantaneous waveform amplitudes, capturing signal power, amplitude envelope dynamics, and waveform zero-crossing behavior without spectral transformation.

Frequency-Domain Spectral Features: Computed via Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), measuring spectral distribution, harmonic energy concentration, and spectral brightness (timbre).

Mathematical Formulations

1. Temporal Short-Time Windowing (Framing)

Given continuous discrete audio signal x[n] sampled at sampling rate Fs, framing extracts a localized window centered at timestamp ti:

Framed Audio Signal Formulation:
xt[n] = x[n] × w[n - nt],     0 ≤ n ≤ N - 1

Where:
• x[n] is the discrete input audio time series.
• w[n] is an analysis window function (e.g., Hamming or rectangular window) of length N.
• nt = ⌊ t × Fs ⌋ is the central discrete sample index for timestamp t.
• N = Fs × Tframe is the total number of samples within the analysis frame.

2. Root Mean Square (RMS) Energy (Feature 1)

Measures instantaneous acoustic signal power and volume magnitude:

RMS Energy Formula:
RMSt = √( ( 1 / N ) × ∑n=0N-1 xt[n]2 )

Where:
• xt[n] is the framed audio sample at index n.
• N is the total number of discrete samples in the analysis frame.
• Technical Objective: Voice Activity Detection (VAD), silence discrimination, loudness metering, and acoustic event onset detection.

3. Zero Crossing Rate (ZCR) (Feature 2)

Quantifies how frequently the audio waveform transitions across the zero-amplitude horizontal axis:

Zero Crossing Rate Formula:
ZCRt = ( 1 / ( 2·N ) ) × ∑n=1N-1 | sgn(xt[n]) - sgn(xt[n - 1]) |

Where:
• sgn(u) = +1 if u ≥ 0, and -1 if u < 0.
• N is the analysis frame length in samples.
• Technical Objective: Differentiating periodic voiced speech (vowels, low ZCR) from high-frequency unvoiced fricatives (/s/, /sh/, high ZCR) and ambient background noise.

4. Spectral Centroid (Feature 3)

Represents the center of mass of the magnitude spectrum, indicating acoustic brightness:

Spectral Centroid Formula:
Ct = ( ∑k=0M-1 fk × |Xt[k]| ) / ( ∑k=0M-1 |Xt[k]| )

Where:
• |Xt[k]| is the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) magnitude at discrete frequency bin k.
• fk = ( k × Fs ) / ( 2·M ) is the physical frequency in Hertz corresponding to bin k.
• M is the number of positive discrete frequency bins (M = N/2 + 1).
• Technical Objective: Timbre characterization, musical instrument identification, audio genre classification, and sound texture analysis.

Algorithmic Methodology & Implementation Steps

  1. Embedded a high-fidelity synthetic WAV audio asset directly into the Flask web interface with interactive HTML5 audio controls.
  2. Divided the continuous audio stream into quasi-stationary temporal frames across 10 discrete timestamps spanning 0.5s to 9.5s.
  3. Implemented mathematical algorithms for Root Mean Square (RMS) Energy, Zero Crossing Rate (ZCR), and Spectral Centroid using pure NumPy and SciPy FFT.
  4. Tabulated extracted multi-feature profiles alongside their rigorous downstream technical justifications.
  5. Deployed interactive Flask dashboard on port 5006 displaying synchronous audio playback, multi-feature trajectory graphs, and statistical summaries.
